## 15 Fresh Work Principles

I revisit and refine my working methods regularly—here’s the latest iteration:

1. Continuously curate high-quality input sources  
2. Preserve independent thinking: actively seek counterexamples and revise judgments with evidence  
3. Identify *one* most important task each day  
4. First choose what’s worth long-term investment—*then* optimize execution efficiency  
5. Clarify strategy, problems, and value judgments—repeatedly, both to yourself and others  
6. Advance steadily along a clear phase-level core theme, review regularly, and explicitly decide what to continue, stop, or start  
7. Define work strategies by purpose and outcome—not by activity or tool  
8. Before automating, first question, delete, and simplify  
9. Practice *correct* “AI First”: (a) Is this problem worth solving? (b) Can AI solve it *better*? (c) Does the result *actually improve* the outcome?  
10. Build a small, functional validation loop early—then scale iteratively  
11. Ground work in frontline facts; diagnose root causes; solve systemic issues via mechanism design  
12. Make AI usage an organization’s default capability—not an exception or add-on  
13. Cultivate mutually beneficial partnerships  
14. Measure AI “employees” by business impact—and relentlessly raise human productivity  
15. Turn every major practice into a reusable, documented output

## Stronger AI Favors White-Hat GEO

During last night’s livestream, someone asked: *As AI grows more capable, won’t GEO become harder?*

My answer:  
**Stronger AI helps white-hat GEO—and hurts black-hat GEO.**  
Staying on the right path isn’t just ethical—it’s strategically sustainable.

**White-hat GEO** means clearly articulating real product advantages, domain expertise, and verified data; structuring content thoughtfully; citing sources transparently; and aligning with AI’s preference for authoritative, traceable information—making it easier for AI systems to discover, understand, and accurately cite your content.

**Black-hat GEO**, by contrast, relies on fabricated claims, fake reviews, synthetic testimonials, impersonation of authorities, or exploiting model weaknesses to bias outputs—then mass-distributing low-quality or false content.

As AI’s reasoning and verification abilities improve, well-evidenced, high-integrity content gains visibility—and becomes *more* resilient to manipulation.

What actually drives volatility in AI-generated answers today? Often, it’s not AI itself—but competitive intensity. The fiercer the industry rivalry, the more fluid the positioning in AI responses.

## A 160,000-Word Knowledge Base

Last night, I spoke with the CEO of a GEO agency.

He shared a story about a major client—a publicly listed company paying over ¥2 million annually—for whom they’ve delivered strong results over several months.

A key lever? Building a *robust knowledge base*.

I asked: *How robust?*  
He replied: *Just the cleaned-up, structured content from the past few months totals 160,000 characters—roughly book-length.*

The payoff? AI-generated GEO content became consistently high-quality, genuinely useful to both AI systems and end users—and produced large volumes of valuable, trustworthy information.

The trade-off? Extremely high coordination overhead. The service is heavy, and the team is exhausted.

I asked: *Is there a way around that?*  
He said: *Not really.* Real enterprise environments are messy—internal teams use conflicting terminology, and alignment alone takes time. Yet this work remains indispensable.

That’s white-hat GEO in action.

## The Enduring Value of Backlinks

Someone asked: *What role do backlinks play in GEO—or in official website GEO?*

For foundational insight, revisit Li Yanhong’s early paper *Toward a Qualitative Search Engine*. Its core method—HVV (Hyperlink Vector Voting)—remains illuminating.

Traditional keyword matching can assess textual relevance—but repeated keyword stuffing doesn’t guarantee quality. HVV introduces *external signals*: whether other pages link to yours, and *how* they describe you in those links.

Think of it like academic citations: a citation doesn’t always mean endorsement—but it signals attention and establishes an external relationship.

Though designed for classic search, HVV’s logic still applies directly to official-site GEO. Google confirms that SEO best practices—including backlink strategy—remain relevant for AI Overviews and AI Mode.

Two key takeaways from the paper:

1. The *existence* of a link signals external attention or reference  
2. The *anchor text* provides a third-party description of your page  

Algorithmically: collect inbound links + anchor text → convert query and anchors into term vectors → compute similarity → aggregate scores per target page.

Related is Google’s PageRank—where HVV emphasizes *“relevance-weighted external descriptions and votes,”* while PageRank emphasizes *“importance passed from authoritative pages.”* In PageRank, a single link from Google’s homepage may outweigh hundreds of thousands of generic links.

Over time, these approaches have converged in principle: both treat links as signals of credibility and contextual meaning.

So yes—backlinks remain vital, whether for Baidu or Google. That’s why the backlink market persists as a real, structural need.

We can understand backlink value at three levels:

1. **Discovery**: Links help crawlers find and map the web—still fundamental for both traditional and AI search  
2. **Understanding**: Anchor text and surrounding context act as third-party summaries—helping AI interpret your page’s intent and scope  
3. **Credibility assessment**: Even without explicit rankings, AI must judge which sources are trustworthy. That *is* ranking logic—just less visible  

For official-site GEO, the central question remains: *How many independent, relevant, and credible third parties cite, describe, or verify you?*  
Effective backlinks improve upstream discovery, comprehension, and candidate selection—indirectly expanding your chances of entering AI search’s evidence pool.

At its core, the function of backlinks hasn’t changed much: they tell search engines—and AI—*how the rest of the web understands, describes, and references you.*

## The Power of Endorsement

Over dinner with friends, we discussed enterprise sales.

I shared a hard-won insight: *No matter how deep your expertise—you’re never as persuasive as a respected person endorsing you.*

This year, I helped close several million-yuan deals. A decisive factor each time? A stronger voice lending credibility and opening doors.

My model for enterprise deal success:  
**Deal = Expertise × Trust × Endorsement × Internal Momentum**

For large clients, choosing a partner isn’t just about capability—it’s about risk mitigation: *Will this go sideways? Can I justify this internally? Who takes ownership if things break? Is this company truly reliable?*

So enterprise selling is partly *risk elimination*.

Expertise answers *“Can you deliver?”*  
A powerful endorsement answers *“Why should I believe you’ll deliver?”*  

The larger the deal, the longer the decision chain, and the more stakeholders involved—the higher the leverage of a credible referral. One sentence from a heavyweight—*“I know this team. They’re trustworthy.”*—can carry more weight than 50 pages of proposals and 10 formal presentations.
